What you get when you download a game

Every music bingo game in this store is a complete, ready-to-run event in one download. There is no subscription, no per-player fee, and no account to keep alive — you buy the game once and you own it for as many nights as you want to run it.

Each music bingo pack includes:

  • 250 randomized bingo cards. Every card is different, so a room of 250 people can all play at once without two players sharing a winning line. Cards are laid out to print correctly in landscape on ordinary letter-size paper — no special stock, no trimming.
  • The callsheet. The host's copy: every answer in play order, so you can confirm a bingo in seconds instead of squinting at a phone.
  • A ready-made Spotify and Apple Music playlist. The songs are already sequenced. You press play and host — you are not building a playlist the afternoon of the show.

How a night actually runs

Play about sixty seconds of each song, long enough for the room to argue about it and mark their cards. Call a Line for the quick first win, 4 Corners as the mid-game round, and a Blackout for the big prize at the end. Most hosts get three rounds out of a single pack, which is a comfortable two to three hours of entertainment.

There is nothing to install and nothing to run on stage besides the music itself. If you can play a playlist and read from a sheet, you can host one of these.

Choosing between a single game and a bundle

Buy a single game if you are testing the format, running a one-off party, or want a specific theme for a specific night — a Halloween event, a staff Christmas party, a themed brewery night.

Buy a bundle if you are running anything on a schedule. The multi-packs group games that work as a series — decades, holidays, travel themes, "things in songs" — and cost meaningfully less per game than buying the same titles one at a time. A venue running weekly needs fresh material more than it needs any one title.

Look at the larger sets or the Gold Club if music bingo is becoming a real part of your business rather than an experiment. That is the point where running out of new games each month becomes the actual constraint.

Which category should I start in?

  • Eras — decade-based games. The safest opening bet for a mixed-age crowd, and the reason "Decades" is the most popular thing in the store.
  • Holidays — seasonal nights that sell themselves, because the calendar does the marketing for you.
  • Pre-made Trivia Shows — presentation-style trivia rather than bingo cards, for hosts who want to run a show from a screen.

A few packs inside Music Bingo Card Downloads — Cover Tunes, Countries, Acronyms — reward deeper music and trivia knowledge rather than a title on the tip of your tongue. Good picks if your regulars have played a lot of music bingo already and want to be beaten for once.

Practical questions before you buy

Do I need a Spotify subscription? You need some way to play the music. The playlists are provided for Spotify and Apple Music; if you use a different service, tunemymusic.com will copy a playlist across for you.

How many cards do I actually need to print? One per player, or one per team if you are running teams — most venues run teams and print far fewer than 250.

Can I run the same game twice? Yes. You own it, and with 250 randomized cards a returning crowd is not playing the same card twice. Most hosts rotate a handful of games across a season.
