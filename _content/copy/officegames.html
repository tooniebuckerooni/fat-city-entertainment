Why a game show beats a team-building exercise

The problem with most corporate entertainment is that it asks people who work together to perform in front of each other. A game show does the opposite: it gives everyone something external to shout at. The quiet people on your team turn out to know an unreasonable amount about 90s television, and the room finds that out together.

That is the whole mechanism. Nobody has to be funny, nobody has to share something personal, and nobody has to do a trust fall. They just have to know whether that was Fleetwood Mac.

Choosing a format for your group

  • Under 30 people — a hosted "Game Show" trivia night works well, because teams stay small enough that everyone on a team actually gets to answer something.
  • 30 to 100 — this is the sweet spot for buzzers and a big screen. Teams of six to eight, a proper leaderboard, and enough of a crowd that the between-round banter lands.
  • Over 100 — music bingo scales better than trivia. Everyone has a card, everyone is engaged on every single song, and you are not waiting on twenty teams to hand in answer sheets.

Custom categories are the part people remember

Any trivia host can ask general knowledge questions. The rounds that actually land at a company event are the ones about the company: how many people have been here more than five years, which office has the most coffee consumption, whose LinkedIn photo is that. We build those in alongside the standard categories — music, sports, TV and entertainment, numbers, and the rest.

Send us the material a week or so out and it goes into the show. It is the single cheapest thing you can do to make a hired event feel like it was made for your team, because it was.

Planning notes

A hosted show needs about two hours of room time for the game itself, plus setup beforehand. Book the room for longer than the show — the half hour after the final scores is where the actual mingling happens, and cutting it short is the most common mistake we see.

If your event has speeches, tell us in advance. It is much better to build the show around them than to stop a game show halfway through so someone can thank the finance team.
