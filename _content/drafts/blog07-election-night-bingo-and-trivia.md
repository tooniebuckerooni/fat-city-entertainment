## How to Run It at a Watch Party

You don't need a stage or a microphone. You need a few things on the coffee table and a host who keeps it light.

**One card per person.** Not per couple, not per household. Everybody plays. Print a few extras for the friend who shows up at 9:30 with a plus-one.

**Something to mark with.** Pens work. Candy corn left over from Halloween works better, and it's the right week for it.

**Snacks within reach.** Election coverage runs late. Put out the stuff that survives sitting there for three hours: popcorn, pretzels, a bowl of whatever. Save the hot food for when the first big call comes in.

**Small prizes.** A line wins a snack pick. A blackout wins the remote. Bragging rights are free and somehow worth the most.

**Play trivia in the slow stretch.** There's always a long gap between the first poll closings and anything interesting. That's your trivia window. Ten questions takes about twenty minutes.

**Keep it about the broadcast, not the result.** This is the one rule. The game is about the anchor's laptop and the confetti on standby, not who's ahead. If a debate starts brewing, call the next square. "Okay, who has 'Path to 218'?" works better than any referee whistle. Everybody in the room is on the same team, and the team is the snack table.

And if anyone at your party still needs to vote, send them to [vote.gov](https://vote.gov/) before the bingo starts.
