# Gold Club CTA Repositioning + Bundle/Cart Upsell Copy

Ready to implement on the new GitHub-hosted site. Gold Club is the #1 revenue product (CA$7,996 over 2 years, +40.9%) and should be the primary CTA nearly everywhere — these variants let it show up in different contexts without feeling repetitive or pushy.

## Site-wide Gold Club CTA variants

Rotate these across nav bar, homepage hero, footer, and blog end-of-post CTAs so it doesn't read as the same line everywhere.

**Nav bar / persistent button (short):**
> Join Gold Club

**Homepage hero (benefit-forward):**
> New playlists every month, zero prep time. Join Music Bingo Gold Club.
> [Explore Gold Club →]

**Blog post closing CTA (host-to-host tone, matches the 3 blog posts already drafted):**
> Tired of building your own playlists? Gold Club gives you a constantly refreshed library across every decade and theme — built and ready to run.
> [Explore Music Bingo Gold Club →]

**Footer (low-key, always-present):**
> Never run the same night twice. [Gold Club →]

**Post-purchase / thank-you page (for Bronze or Pro buyers — upsell path):**
> You've got your first pack — Gold Club keeps them coming. New themes every month, one subscription.
> [Upgrade to Gold Club →]

**Subscription framing test (per the 30-day list's "test subscription/annual framing" item):** A/B two variants against each other once the new site is live:
- Variant A (monthly framing): "$X/month for a new pack every month"
- Variant B (annual framing): "$X/year — less than the cost of one bar tab, covers every night this year"
Track conversion rate on each for at least 4-6 weeks before declaring a winner; at this store's volume (16-61 orders/month), a shorter test window won't be statistically meaningful.

## Bundle upsell copy (cart page)

**When cart contains a single decade or genre pack (e.g., The 80s, The 90s):**
> Building a full rotation? Bundle 3 packs and save — or skip the picking entirely with [Gold Club], where every pack is included.

**When cart contains Bingo Card Generator Pro:**
> Pro gets you unlimited card sets. Pair it with a [Music Bingo Bronze pack] to run your first themed night tonight.

**When cart contains Bronze pack:**
> Loved Bronze? [Gold Club] gives you a new pack like this every month for less than buying them one at a time.

## Cart CTA copy (checkout button variants)
Keep the actual checkout button action-oriented and low-friction:
> Complete My Order (default)
> Get My Packs (alternate, more casual tone matching the brand voice)

Avoid generic "Submit" or "Checkout" — per CTA best practice, specific action language converts better and matches the practical, host-to-host tone used across the blog content.

## "Complete Your Night" bundle CTA (generator + blog pages)

This directly addresses the 30-day list item on plugging the ~1.7% view-to-cart leak. Place this as a card/box on the free generator's results page and at the end of each blog post, distinct from the plain Gold Club CTA:

**Headline:** Complete Your Night
**Body:** You've got your cards — now grab a themed pack to match. Bundle any 2 packs and save, or go all-in with Gold Club for a new one every month.
**Buttons:** [Shop Packs] [Explore Gold Club]

This gives visitors two paths (one-time bundle vs. subscription) rather than forcing a single choice, which should help lift the weak 1.7% view-to-cart rate without over-committing casual first-time visitors to a subscription right away.

## Implementation notes
- Keep Gold Club CTA copy consistent in wording across the 3 blog posts already delivered (they currently use "[Explore Music Bingo Gold Club]" as the placeholder — swap in the "Blog post closing CTA" variant above for consistency) and the new site's templates.
- Do not stack more than one CTA style per page section — per CTA best practice, one primary action per page/section keeps conversion clean.
- Re-run the subscription framing A/B test seasonally — framing that wins in the Sep-Oct trough may not win during the Jan peak when purchase intent is different (see the data-findings memo on why seasonal assumptions here should be tested, not assumed).
