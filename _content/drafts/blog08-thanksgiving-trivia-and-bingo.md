## Family Table vs Friendsgiving

These are two different rooms, and they want two different hosts.

**At the family table,** keep it gentle. You've got three generations and a dog under the table. Play in teams by household or by side of the table, so nobody gets singled out. Read questions slowly. Let Aunt Carol have the answer she nearly got. The goal is everyone laughing at the same time, which at some family dinners is a minor miracle.

**At Friendsgiving,** you can turn it up. Teams of three or four, a scorekeeper with a whiteboard or the back of a takeout menu, and real stakes: losers do the dishes. Friends will heckle. Let them. Add a house rule that a wrong answer has to be defended out loud for ten seconds. It's the best part of the night.

## The Kids' Version

Kids are the best trivia players at the table because they have no shame about guessing. A few ways to bring them in:

- **Pair each kid with a grown-up** and let the kid be the one who says the answer out loud.
- **Read it, then repeat it.** Kids lose a question halfway through. Say it twice.
- **Give partial points** for a good guess. "A pilgrim hat" is not the answer, but it deserves something.
- **Hand them the bingo cards first.** Bingo is perfect for under-tens. Let them call it the moment they get a line, even if they're wrong. Especially if they're wrong.

## How to Run It in 20 Minutes Between Dinner and Pie

You don't need a whole evening. You need the window between the last plate cleared and the pie coming out of the oven.

1. **Before dinner:** print the host sheet and the bingo cards. Stack them by the pie plates so you can't forget.
2. **Plates cleared:** announce it. "Pie's in twenty minutes. Trivia until then." Nobody argues with a deadline that ends in pie.
3. **Split into teams** and hand out paper and pens. Two minutes, tops.
4. **Read the ten questions.** About a minute each. Keep moving. Don't let one question become a debate.
5. **Score it while the pie gets sliced.** Winners pick their slice first. That's the prize.
6. **Bingo with dessert,** if the crowd is still up for it. One quick game while everyone eats.

That's it. Twenty minutes, nobody on the couch, and a story to tell at next year's dinner.
