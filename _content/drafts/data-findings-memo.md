# Data Findings: Exact Weebly Export (Jul 1, 2024 – Jul 22, 2026)

Pulled directly from the Weebly store dashboard (read-only, no changes made). Two findings here materially update the original strategy doc, so read this before acting further on seasonality or the Decades pack.

## 0. Housekeeping notes
- **Currency:** Weebly reports store sales in **CAD**, not USD. All figures below (and in the original 2-year totals) are CAD. Worth confirming with your payment processor whether checkout is actually charging customers in CAD or USD — if the site is charging USD but the report is CAD, your real USD revenue is different from what's displayed.
- **Totals independently verified:** I re-summed all 25 months of exact daily/monthly data myself and it ties out exactly to the headline totals: $28,049.36 sales, 693 orders, 1,048 units. The funnel numbers also match exactly: 65,635 page views → 1,115 added to cart → 693 purchased. The data is clean and trustworthy — the issue is with how the *seasonality* was characterized, not the underlying numbers.

**What this actually shows, month by month across both years:**

- **September is not a consistent trough.** Sep 2024 ($1,870.50) was the second-highest sales month in the entire 2-year dataset. Sep 2025 ($457.46) was the single lowest month in the entire dataset. Same calendar month, opposite extremes.
- **January is not the peak.** Jan 2025 ($1,814.50) was strong — but Jan 2026 ($784.50) was the third-lowest month of the whole period. In *orders*, January was actually one of the weakest months both years (27 and 19 orders — lower than November or December both years).
- **December holds up reasonably well as a strong month for orders** (61 orders in 2024, 45 in 2025 — both among the highest of their respective years), even though sales-dollar peaks are noisier because a few larger purchases (Gold Club renewals, bulk school/bar orders) can swing a month.
- **August is the one point of real consistency on the low end** — Aug 2024 ($716, 22 orders) and Aug 2025 ($504, 20 orders) were both soft, which lines up with the "smaller mid-summer dip" the original doc mentioned.
- **The April–May "secondary bump" is inconsistent too.** Apr 2025 (20 orders) was one of the weakest months of that year; Apr 2026 (31 orders) was one of the strongest. May behaves similarly erratically.

**Why this matters for the campaign plan:** At roughly 20-60 orders a month, this store is small enough that a handful of larger orders (a school buying multiple packs, a bar chain renewing Gold Club) can swing a month's total by 30-50%. What reads as a clean seasonal wave on a compressed dashboard chart is, at this volume, mostly noise plus a couple of real signals (December strength, a summer soft patch). I'd treat "Sep-Oct acquisition, Jan-conversion" as a reasonable default marketing cadence to *try*, not as a proven seasonal pattern to bet the whole plan on. The Sep-Oct campaign plan already delivered is still a sound idea (cheap acquisition during a historically softer stretch, banking a list for later), but I'd frame it internally as a hypothesis you're testing this year, not a confirmed cycle — and the Week 6 check-in in that plan becomes more important, not less.

## 2. "Decades" pack investigation: it's not an isolated product problem

Full 2-year, item-level data pulled from Weebly's Items report:

| Product | Sales (CAD) | vs. prior 752-day period | Units |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Decades" Music Bingo 5-Pack (the bundle) | $1,302.62 | **-47.34%** | 32 |
| The 60s - 250 Music Bingo Cards | $47.20 | **-71.36%** | 5 |
| The 70s - 250 Music Bingo Cards | $323.80 | **-44.00%** | 28 |
| The 80s - 250 Music Bingo Cards | $615.10 | **-24.63%** | 58 |
| The 90s - 250 Music Bingo Cards | $431.20 | **-25.78%** | 36 |
| The 2000s - 250 Music Bingo Cards | $514.35 | +6.70% | 42 |
| 90s R&B - 250 Music Bingo Cards | $412.55 | +3,200% (new, tiny base) | 34 |
| Golden Oldies - 250 Music Bingo Cards | $569.48 | -17.74% | 61 |

**The finding:** every broad single-decade pack is declining — not just the "Decades" bundle. The 60s is down 71%, the 70s down 44%, the 80s down 25%, the 90s down 26%, Golden Oldies down 18%. The only decade-adjacent products growing are The 2000s (+6.7%, essentially flat) and a brand-new niche spinoff, "90s R&B" (still in its early small-base growth phase, so the huge percentage isn't yet meaningful in dollar terms).

This changes the diagnosis. The original ask was "why did the Decades bundle decline 47%" — but the real pattern is that **broad, generic decade content across the board is losing ground**, while newer, more specific sub-niche packs (90s R&B) and current-feeling content (2000s, Christmas Party at +16%, the subscription clubs at +30-41%) are growing. That's consistent with the angle already built into Blog Post #2 ("Games Our Crowds Can't Get Enough Of") — but it suggests the fix isn't just "revive interest in Decades," it's "the catalog may need more sub-niche packs like 90s R&B rather than more broad-decade packs." Worth considering a couple more specific spinoffs (e.g., "80s New Wave," "70s Yacht Rock," "2010s Pop") as a catalog experiment, separate from the content/marketing fixes already underway.

Two things worth flagging beyond what the original doc said:
- **Reddit isn't a single lucky referral — it's a live, growing referring source right now** (48 + 4 views this month, +50% growth). That's a stronger case for the campaign's Reddit investment than "one strong referral in the past" implied.
- **"aio.online" is a real and growing referrer** (25 views, +76%). That domain pattern (AI answer engines / AI aggregators) suggests FatCity content is starting to get surfaced by an AI-powered search/answer tool — an emerging channel worth watching as those tools grow, and a reason to make sure blog content is structured clearly (direct answers, clear headers) since that's exactly what these tools tend to lift.

## Bottom line
The core 30-day plan doesn't need to be thrown out, but two adjustments are worth making: treat the Sep-Oct/Jan seasonal framing as a working hypothesis rather than settled fact (revisit at the Week 6 check-in with real data), and reframe the Decades investigation from "fix one declining product" to "broad decade packs are fading across the board — lean into niche spinoffs like 90s R&B instead of trying to revive the generic bundle."
