# Weekly Host Email — Reusable Template

Purpose: this is the recurring "new playlist/pack" email from the 30-day list, aimed at the 11.9% stickiness problem — most subscribers/customers see FatCity once and don't come back. A consistent weekly touchpoint keeps the brand top-of-mind between purchases and gives Gold Club a natural, recurring showcase.

## Cadence and segmentation
- **Send weekly, same day/time** (pick one and stick to it — consistency matters more than the specific day for a "weekly" email to feel reliable).
- **Segment if your email platform supports it:** generator-signups (no purchase yet) should see more educational/hosting-tip content and softer product mentions; existing Bronze/Pro/Gold Club customers can see more direct new-pack announcements since they've already shown purchase intent.

## Template structure

**Subject line formula (rotate these patterns week to week):**
- "This week's pack: [Theme Name]"
- "New: [Theme Name] is live"
- "Your Thursday night just got easier"
- "[Number] songs your crowd already knows"

**Preview text:** One line teasing the specific pack or tip inside — never repeat the subject line.

**Header/hero:** Pack cover image + one-line value statement, e.g. "Fresh 90s R&B pack, ready to run tonight."

**Body block 1 — This week's featured content:**
> [Pack name] just dropped. [1-2 sentences on why it works — recognizable songs, a specific vibe, who it's best for.]
> [Get This Pack →] or, for Gold Club members: [It's already in your library →]

**Body block 2 — One practical tip (rotate through a short list so it doesn't repeat):**
> Quick tip: [pull from the hosting guide — e.g., "Start advertising 2 weeks out, not 2 days. It's the single biggest lever for turnout."]

**Body block 3 — Social proof or community touch (once social channels are active):**
> This week's biggest reaction: [link to or embed a crowd-reaction video clip, if available]. Tag us if you post your own night — we love reposting hosts' clips.

**Primary CTA:** One clear action — either "Get This Week's Pack" (for non-subscribers) or "See What's New in Gold Club" (for subscribers). Never both as equally weighted buttons; pick the one that matches the segment.

**Footer:** Unsubscribe link, social links, FatCityEntertainment.com

## Sample filled-in email

**Subject:** This week's pack: 90s R&B

**Preview text:** The genre pack that's already outperforming full-decade rounds.

**Body:**

Hey [First Name],

This week we're spotlighting **90s R&B** — a genre round that's been quietly one of our fastest-growing packs. If your crowd skews toward "everyone knows the chorus" energy over deep-decade nostalgia, this is the one to try next.

**[Get the 90s R&B Pack →]**

Quick hosting tip: genre rounds work best as your *second* round of the night, once the room's already warmed up on a broader opener. Save your most specific theme for when people are already playing.

Running a themed night this week? Tag us — we love seeing (and sharing) your crowd's reactions.

See you next week,
The FatCity Entertainment Team

## Notes for implementation
- Build a rolling content calendar of at least 8-10 weeks of themes/tips in advance so this doesn't become a weekly scramble — pull directly from the product catalog and the hosting-tip list already established in Blog 1.
- Track open rate and click rate per segment from week one; if generator-signups consistently underperform existing customers, revisit the educational-vs-promotional balance for that segment.
- This is the natural home for the Decades-category finding from the data memo: use upcoming weeks to spotlight niche spinoff packs (90s R&B, and future ones like it) rather than the broad single-decade packs that are declining.
